# Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement and Work-for-Hire Acknowledgement

**Template prepared:** 2026-05-02
**For use by:** Blank Canvas, Inc.
**Disclaimer:** This is a working template, not an attorney-reviewed final document. Have a qualified attorney review it before use in any high-stakes engagement (employee hiring, investor pitch, large contractor work). For low-stakes engagements (a single freelancer reviewing a specific file, a feedback session with an advisor), this template is reasonable as-is.

This document is **two agreements in one**:

- **Part A — Mutual NDA:** both parties agree to keep each other's confidential information secret.
- **Part B — Work-for-Hire and IP Assignment:** if the receiving party produces any work product based on or contributing to the disclosing party's project, all such work product is owned by the disclosing party.

Sign once, covers both.

## How to Use

1. Fill in the bracketed fields below.

## Part A — Mutual Non-Disclosure

### A.1. Confidential Information

"Confidential Information" means any non-public information disclosed by the Company to the Recipient, in any form (written, oral, electronic, visual, or otherwise), including without limitation:

- The source code, design documents, rulebooks, audit data, calibration tables, AI parameter values, simulation results, frequency tables, and trade-secret items associated with the project known as "Yellow Jacket Tour" or any successor or related project of the Company.
- The contents of files including but not limited to `index.html`, `RULES.md`, `MIXED_GAMES_DESIGN.md`, audit CSVs, and any internal documents or notes shared with the Recipient.
- Any business plans, financial information, marketing strategies, customer lists, or commercial terms communicated to the Recipient.
- The fact and substance of any discussions between the Company and the Recipient regarding the project.

Confidential Information **excludes**:

(a) information that is or becomes publicly available through no fault of the Recipient;
(b) information already lawfully known to the Recipient prior to disclosure (the Recipient must produce written evidence of prior knowledge to invoke this exception);
(c) information independently developed by the Recipient without reference to or use of any Confidential Information (with written evidence);
(d) information lawfully received from a third party not under any duty of confidence to the Company.

### A.2. Obligations of the Recipient

The Recipient shall:

(a) hold all Confidential Information in strict confidence;
(b) use Confidential Information only for the purpose of the engagement described in §A.6 below ("Permitted Purpose"), and for no other purpose;
(c) not disclose Confidential Information to any third party without the Company's prior written consent;
(d) protect Confidential Information with at least the same degree of care that the Recipient uses for its own confidential information of similar importance, but in no case less than reasonable care;
(e) not copy, reproduce, reverse-engineer, decompile, or create derivative works of any Confidential Information except as strictly necessary for the Permitted Purpose;
(f) on the Company's request, return or destroy all copies of Confidential Information in the Recipient's possession or control within 7 days of the request, and certify such return or destruction in writing.

### A.3. Compelled Disclosure

If the Recipient is compelled by law (court order, subpoena, valid government request) to disclose any Confidential Information, the Recipient shall:

(a) promptly notify the Company in writing before disclosure (unless legally prohibited from doing so);
(b) cooperate with the Company in any effort to obtain a protective order or other limit on the disclosure;
(c) disclose only the minimum portion of Confidential Information legally required.

### A.4. No License

Nothing in this Agreement grants the Recipient any license, right, title, or interest in any Confidential Information, intellectual property, or other proprietary rights of the Company. All such rights remain solely with the Company.

### A.5. Reciprocity

To the extent the Recipient discloses confidential information of their own to the Company in the course of the engagement (such as the Recipient's professional methods, prior work product, or business information), the Company assumes the same obligations under §§A.2–A.4 with respect to such information, mutatis mutandis.

## Part B — Work-for-Hire and IP Assignment

### B.1. Definitions

"Work Product" means any and all materials, work, ideas, inventions, designs, source code, documentation, audit data, parameter values, calibration tables, mechanic descriptions, names, marks, artwork, audio, lore, or other deliverables that the Recipient creates, develops, contributes to, or assists in creating, in the course of the engagement, that:

(a) are based on or derived from the Company's Confidential Information; or
(b) relate to the Company's project Yellow Jacket Tour or any successor or related project; or
(c) are produced using the Company's resources, time, or facilities.

### B.2. Work-for-Hire

All Work Product is hereby characterized as "work made for hire" within the meaning of the U.S. Copyright Act, 17 U.S.C. § 101, to the maximum extent permitted by law. The Company is the sole author and owner of all Work Product.

### B.3. Assignment

To the extent any Work Product does not qualify as a "work made for hire" by operation of law, the Recipient hereby irrevocably assigns and transfers to the Company all right, title, and interest in and to such Work Product, including without limitation all copyright, patent rights, trademark rights, trade-secret rights, moral rights (to the extent waivable), and all other intellectual-property rights, throughout the world, in perpetuity.

### B.4. Cooperation

The Recipient shall, at the Company's reasonable request and at the Company's expense (for any out-of-pocket costs), execute any documents, perform any acts, and provide any reasonable cooperation necessary to perfect, enforce, or defend the Company's rights in the Work Product. This obligation survives termination of the engagement.

### B.5. Recipient's Pre-existing IP

If the Recipient incorporates into any Work Product any pre-existing intellectual property of the Recipient (such as a generic library, framework, or method developed independently before this engagement), the Recipient grants the Company a perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free, sublicensable license to use, modify, distribute, and create derivative works of such pre-existing IP solely as incorporated into the Work Product. The Recipient shall identify any such pre-existing IP in writing at the time of incorporation; otherwise it is presumed assigned under §B.3.

### B.6. No Assignment of Recipient's Unrelated IP

Nothing in §§B.1–B.5 assigns to the Company any of the Recipient's intellectual property that does not constitute Work Product as defined in §B.1.

## Appendix — When NOT to Use This Template Without Attorney Review

This template is appropriate for: brief contractor reviews, advisor conversations, single-deliverable freelance work, friend-and-family feedback sessions.

It is **not** appropriate (without attorney review) for:

- Hiring an employee. Use a full employment agreement with §B.2/B.3 incorporated, plus state-specific labor-law provisions.
- Investor pitches with sophisticated counterparties. They will negotiate the terms and your starting position should be drafted by counsel.
- Engagements with parties in non-US jurisdictions. The §C.4 Wyoming-law clause will need to be re-thought.
- Engagements involving pre-existing rights of the Recipient that they refuse to assign. §B.5 needs careful negotiation.
- Anything where the engagement value exceeds ~$10,000 or the IP risk is material to the business.

For any of the above, spend the $500–1,500 to have a corporate attorney review and revise.
